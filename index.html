The real cost of outsourcing

What do you actually lose when printing goes outside?

You lose 4 things, and only 1 of them shows up on the invoice.

LOSS 1

The date

Your client moves the delivery forward. Your vendor moves it back. You are the one on the phone with both.

LOSS 2

The design

Every artwork file you send out sits on a machine you do not control. The vendor also prints for the buyer who lost to you.

LOSS 3

The order

The vendor's minimum is 50 pieces. Your customer wants 12. So you quote high and hope they say no.

LOSS 4

The rework

One wrong spelling costs you the product, the courier both ways, the vendor's queue position and the date you promised.

Skilled hand labour does not fix this. As one factory owner told us: it is very difficult to get skilled labour, and even when we get it, they cannot do enough production, so we are unable to take big export orders.

How should you compare 2 garment printing machine quotes?

Compare the 7 things below, in this order. Most buyers compare price first, sign, and then discover that 6 of these were never in the quote.

1. Service response timeAsk in writing: how many hours to an engineer at my address, and how many engineers are in my state?
2. Spare and head availabilityIs a replacement print head in stock in India today, or does it ship from China when yours fails?
3. Consumables supplyWho supplies ink, film and powder after month 3, and at what price?
4. Print head type and countHead model and quantity set both speed and repair cost. Ask for the model name, not "industrial head".
5. Real output, not peak outputPeak speed is quoted at the fastest pass. Ask for output at the pass setting you will actually run.
6. Power and spaceA shaker can pull 7000W at 35A. Confirm the point and the floor area before delivery, not after.
7. Sampling before paymentWill they print your design on your material before you release the balance?

Which is better for a garment unit, DTF or screen printing?

DTF wins when your orders are small, mixed and full colour, because there is no screen, no plate and no setup cost per design. Screen printing still wins on very long runs of one single-colour design. If your average order is under 200 pieces with more than 3 colours, DTF is almost always the cheaper route.
